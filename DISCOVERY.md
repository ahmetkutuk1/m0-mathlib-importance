## Detailed Findings

### 1. Central Limit Theorem (The Strongest Candidate)
- **Status:** Infrastructure is highly mature. Mathlib has `ProbabilityTheory.IsGaussian` and related measure theory.
- **Evidence:** The neighborhood contains `MeasureTheory.integral_const` (M=417) and `MeasureTheory.integral_nonneg` (M=404). 
- **Gaussian Specifics:** Filtering for `IsGaussian` reveals the core lemmas:
  - `isGaussian_of_charFunDual_eq` (M: 259)
  - `IsGaussian.charFun_eq` (M: 257)
  - `isGaussian_conv` (M: 264)
- **Verdict:** The mathematical machinery is ready. A formalization of the Central Limit Theorem would sit at the very top of the M_0 hierarchy.

### 2. Geometry Theorems (Ceva, Morley, Pick)
- **Status:** Genuinely missing as named theorems.
- **Evidence:** The `EuclideanGeometry` field has a relatively low M_0 ceiling (max M ≈ 272 for `angle`). 
- **Verdict:** The foundational Euclidean geometry library is still shallow. Formalizing these theorems requires expanding the geometry infrastructure first.

### 3. Transcendental Numbers (π and Hermite-Lindemann)
- **Status:** Missing, but our automated keyword search produced false positives.
- **Note:** The keyword `transcendental` matched field-specific algebraic definitions (e.g., `Algebra.transcendental`) rather than real/complex transcendentals. A manual search for `Real.pi` or `Complex.exp` is required.

## Correction / Update (Post-Analysis)

**Update on Central Limit Theorem (CLT):** 
Following this analysis, a manual cross-check of the Mathlib repository revealed that the CLT *has already been formalized* in `Mathlib.Probability.CentralLimitTheorem` (e.g., `ProbabilityTheory.tendstoInDistribution_inv_sqrt_mul_sum`). 

**Why did the Discovery Engine miss this?**
The M_0 graph dataset (MathNetwork/MathlibGraph) used in this analysis likely did not capture the edges for this specific file, or the theorem names did not match our `IsGaussian` neighborhood heuristic. This serves as a crucial limitation of graph-based discovery: **absence of evidence in the graph is not evidence of absence in the codebase.** Future versions of the M_0 engine will incorporate a direct `#check` scan of the Lean environment to prevent such false positives.

*This correction ensures the scientific integrity of the M_0 project.*
